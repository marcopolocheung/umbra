# Prove Umbra's shade before training anything

Almost every ML component Umbra could plausibly add already exists as mature, published, usually open work with error bars attached. That covers canopy height, building heights, shadow detection, sky-view and greenery indices, SOLWEIG thermal-comfort emulators, photo geolocation, learned ETA, route-preference learning from GPS traces, and assistant long-term memory. Umbra's core feature is also commoditized. **shadewalker.nyc already routes shade across 488,677 NYC sidewalk edges in all five boroughs** ([GitHub](https://github.com/camrynobscura/shadewalker)), openrouteservice ships shaded routing in **136 European cities** ([HeiGIT](https://heigit.org/shadow-data-for-climate-resilient-urban-planning/)), and a "Prefer shade" toggle sits unlaunched in the Google Maps APK ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-maps-sun-shade-apk-teardown-3613515/)). What remains open is narrow, and most of it is evaluation. No shade router publishes field accuracy. No one has measured how much pricing sun exposure at each segment's traversal time, rather than at departure, changes routes. No product learns a walker's shade preference from their choices, and the only public choice dataset holds 408 decisions. No open tool automates photo chronolocation against rendered building shadows. Practitioners point the same way: a baseline to beat, error analysis, self-collected data and shipped latency count for far more than architecture choice. The best sequence is therefore to land two or three cheap non-ML foundations first: a LiDAR shadow audit, a traversal-time ablation, and a pass^k reliability report for the assistant. Then add one small trained model that is genuinely Umbra's, a Bayesian shade-preference model wired into assistant memory, with semi-automatic shadow chronolocation as the demo-able stretch. A canopy model, a shadow detector, a geolocation model or a vector-database memory would each rebuild something that is already finished.

## Mature ML already covers most of what Umbra could train

Two patterns run through the catalogue below. First, the geospatial projects that earn respect publish **per-region error metrics and known failure modes**, not just a model. Microsoft's building footprints report precision and recall by continent, for example 94.3% / 85.9% for Europe ([GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/GlobalMLBuildingFootprints)). Google's Open Buildings 2.5D reports a **1.5 m height MAE but evaluated only in North America, Europe and Japan**, while the dataset itself covers only the Global South, so it has no NYC data at all ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/open-buildings-25d-temporal-dataset-tracks-building-changes-across-the-global-south/)). Meta/WRI's 1 m canopy-height map has a **2.8 m MAE** ([Meta](https://sustainability.atmeta.com/blog/2024/04/22/using-artificial-intelligence-to-map-the-earths-forests/)), but an independent 15-city urban check put its canopy precision and recall at only **about 69% at the 1 m point scale** ([UF&UG 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1618866726002438)). Data with uncertainty attached is worth more than a model without it, and that is the bar any Umbra number will be read against.

Second, the closest prior art is infrastructure, not accuracy. Umbra's closest prior art, ShadeMap, fits the infrastructure pattern exactly: one developer, about four years of work, a WebGL shader that ray-traces terrain, building and tree shadows worldwide, and several Show HN launches ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40528045)). Umbra's differentiation therefore cannot come from shadow rendering. It has to come from routing, transit, rain shelter, the assistant, and evaluation.

## Prior art for the four candidate ideas, and where each gap actually sits

### Shade routing is commoditized; time-dependent pricing and learned preference are not

Shade-weighted Dijkstra over OSM with a precomputed shade field has at least ten implementations, a continental deployment and academic variants. CoolWalks studies it in Manhattan, Barcelona and Valencia, and finds that on a regular grid with uniform building heights the shade weight brings **no benefit over the shortest path**. Gains come only from irregular geometry ([Sci. Reports 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97200-2)). That finding should shape Umbra's demos: Midtown's grid is the worst place to show the feature off. Thermal-comfort routing (mean radiant temperature, UTCI) is academically active but compute-bound. ASU's Cool Routes uses SOLWEIG MRT, cut average MRT by up to 3.8 °C with detours under 3% ([Building & Environment 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132326004270)), and says city-scale coverage needs "many hours" of compute and more efficient AI models ([ASU News](https://news.asu.edu/20260610-environment-and-sustainability-new-cool-routes-app-maps-shadiest-routes-your-walk)).

| Competitor | What it ships | How it handles time | Validation |
|---|---|---|---|
| [shadewalker.nyc](https://github.com/camrynobscura/shadewalker) (AGPL, 2026) | All five boroughs, 488,677 edges, building shadows every 5 m for every daylight hour of every month, seasonal tree canopy, 4 shade weights per request | **Priced at departure time** | Software tests + route-regression goldens |
| [ShadeMap](https://shademap.app/shadeprofile/) | Shadow map, Shade Profile and GPX replay for a fixed route | Time-varying analysis, no route optimization | Author calls it "just an estimate" ([blog](https://tedpiotrowski.svbtle.com/sun-and-shadow-maps-models-vs-reality)) |
| [openrouteservice HEAL](https://heigit.org/shadow-data-for-climate-resilient-urban-planning/) | 136 European cities, LiDAR heights + 1 m canopy | 4 fixed slots (09/12/15/18h) | No published metrics |
| [Google Maps "Prefer shade"](https://www.androidauthority.com/google-maps-sun-shade-apk-teardown-3613515/) | Walking toggle with "minutes in sun" strings | Unknown | Teardown only; not launched as of Oct 2026 |
| [ASU Cool Routes](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-cool-routes-cooler-paths-hourly.html) | MRT-cost routing, Tempe campus only; app promised 2027 | Hourly forecast slot | MRT validated against the MaRTy cart (d = 0.73) |
| [Barcelona Cool Walks](https://cool.bcnregional.com/) | Shortest / shady / "vampire" modes, trees, fountains | Not documented | None published |
| [Coway Map](https://www.cowaymap.com/) (Singapore) | Sheltered-walkway rain routing + transit | Live rainfall | None published |
| [ShadeWalk](https://shadewalk.fit/), [shade-engine](https://github.com/aJustDev/shade-engine), [Shadeway (NYC)](https://github.com/HenryZhao88/Shadeway) and ~6 more hobby repos | Weighted Dijkstra/A* variants | Departure time or canonical solar instants | None |

Against this field, Umbra's defensible differences are the Pareto set (no shade tool found returns one rather than a fixed weight ladder), transit legs, and the rain objective. Coway Map already covers rain-sheltered transit routing in Singapore, so rain shelter is not unique either. The genuine research gap is **time**. Only one paper treats minimum-sun walking as time-dependent routing: Li, Yoshimura, Tu and Ratti precomputed exposure every 5 minutes from Street View segmentation in Shibuya and reported **35.23% less exposure than shortest paths** over 1,000 OD pairs. Their evaluation was simulation only, with no static-vs-time-dependent comparison and no detour lengths ([arXiv 1910.04312](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04312)). CoolWalks holds time constant per route, MIT's Dubai study uses one-hour windows ([MIT Senseable](https://senseable.mit.edu/papers/pdf/20250911_Wen-etal_WalkingSmart_CEUS.pdf)), and shadewalker prices at departure. No source quantifies how wrong departure-time pricing is, and Umbra's own README names this as an admitted limitation.

The algorithmic side is easier than it looks, though this reasoning comes from the notes and needs checking. A walker at constant speed has time-independent travel time, so FIFO holds trivially. Looking up shade at τ = t₀ + d/v during a label-setting search is then exact for a weighted-sum cost. For pure walking Pareto search, arrival time is a function of distance, so dominance still works. That breaks once a subway leg inserts schedule-dependent waiting, which is exactly Umbra's multimodal case. Heavy machinery such as time-dependent contraction hierarchies is unnecessary. The real cost is the shade-field lookup at many timestamps. No open-source engine (OSRM, Valhalla, GraphHopper, OTP) ships time-varying pedestrian comfort costs. OTP passes wall-clock time into street-edge traversal and is the nearest forkable path ([OTP docs](https://docs.opentripplanner.org/en/latest/RouteRequest/)).

Preference learning is the other open lane. The only controlled revealed-choice experiment is Melnikov et al.: **46 people, 408 binary choices** in a Singapore courtyard. Its hierarchical logit gives a population sun aversion of β̄ = 1.16 (100 m of shade feels like 86 m of sun), individual values of about 0.37–1.84, and tree shade half as effective as building shade. The **data and code are public on OSF** ([PMC8844002](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8844002/); [osf.io/aj4vk](https://osf.io/aj4vk/)). MIT's Dubai study borrowed it for lack of local data. No GPS route-choice model with shade covariates was found, and no product learns preference online: shadewalker uses fixed weights 0/5/15/40 and Barcelona three modes. A cautionary field result also applies: in Trondheim, participants **seldom chose** the weather-avoiding route even when offered ([MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijgi10060365)). Stated comfort preferences may be weaker than designers assume.

Learned ETA (Google's GNN, Uber DeepETA), world-scale IRL, and grid-based Neural A* are mature or mismatched. Walking ETA is distance over speed, Umbra has no traces, and Neural A* is grid-only. Learned multi-objective search is thin: one September 2026 preprint learns a few linear cost functions whose shortest paths condense an exact Pareto set ([arXiv 2609.19996](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19996v1)). Exact bi-objective search already handles **NYC-scale road graphs (264k nodes)** quickly ([OPMOS/LCDPF](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3721145.3725781)), so a learned heuristic would buy Umbra little latency.

### Photo-to-place is a commodity; photo-to-minute via rendered shadows is a narrow open gap

Coarse photo geolocation is solved for practical purposes. Gemini-2.5-pro reaches a **4.2 km median error and 92.4% country accuracy** on one benchmark ([arXiv 2508.01608](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01608v2)). PIGEON puts 40.36% of Street View guesses within 25 km ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Haas_PIGEON_Predicting_Image_Geolocations_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)). Street-level accuracy on generic photos remains low, about **14–24% within 1 km** on Im2GPS3k and YFCC4k for GeoCLIP, G3 and similar methods ([GeoMetric comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08918)). For an NYC-only app the city is already known, so planet-scale geolocation adds nothing. Block-level position in a known city has three mature families. Retrieval place recognition reaches about 94% R@1 on Pitts30k ([MegaLoc](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17237)) but needs a hosted NYC image index. Structure-based localization (hloc) needs an SfM model. OrienterNet matches a photo against **2D OpenStreetMap** with meter-level recall inside a local tile, given a coarse GPS prior ([CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/html/Sarlin_OrienterNet_Visual_Localization_in_2D_Public_Maps_With_Neural_Matching_CVPR_2023_paper.html)). Only OrienterNet fits Umbra's data. It is Python, and a browser port has unknown operator support. Licensing removes the obvious shortcut. Google Maps Platform terms forbid indexing Street View or using it to "train, test, validate or fine-tune" models ([Maps Platform Terms](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms)). Mapillary and KartaView imagery is CC BY-SA 4.0 ([Mapillary](https://help.mapillary.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001770409-CC-BY-SA-license-for-open-data)), and StreetCLIP's weights are non-commercial ([model card](https://huggingface.co/geolocal/StreetCLIP/blob/main/README.md)).

Time is where Umbra's geometry matters. Learned estimators are coarse. GT-Loc, the best purely visual method found, has an hour error of **2.72 h** and a month error of 1.40 months ([ICCV 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10473)). VLMs on the TimeSpot benchmark reportedly sit near **4 h MAE** ([TimeSpot](https://huggingface.co/datasets/kagnlp/TimeSpot); figure from a search summary, not verified on the page). Geometric measurement with a known place and a measurable shadow is far better: a Bellingcat analyst measured a 2.33 m object's 3.53 m shadow in SunCalc and estimated **09:37 against a true 09:43**, a single anecdote rather than a benchmark ([Bellingcat](https://www.bellingcat.com/resources/2021/05/18/unsure-when-a-video-or-photo-was-taken-how-to-tell-by-measuring-the-length-of-shadows/)). Known geometry closes a roughly 20–30× accuracy gap, and Umbra holds the geometry.

The idea of rendering shadows at candidate times and comparing them to a photo is not novel. Bellingcat's toolkit documents doing it manually with ShadeMap and Shadowmap ([toolkit](https://bellingcat.gitbook.io/toolkit/more/all-tools/shademap)). Patents describe re-casting 3D-model shadows to match images ([US 9892550](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9892550)). An MDPI paper solved for sun position from a single roof corner and its shadow against a 3D city model ([Remote Sens. 11(1):72](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/11/1/72)). Geometric shadow-trajectory geolocation dates to Junejo and Foroosh, ECCV 2008 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-88682-2_25)). What appears unbuilt is an **open, automated, browser-based optimizer** that registers a ground-level photo to footprint shadows and searches over time. ShadowTrace inverts the problem, inferring location from a known time ([GitHub](https://github.com/bytemallet/shadowtrace)). The novelty is in execution, and any write-up should frame it as "automating the ShadeMap chronolocation workflow". Three hard limits apply. Registering the photo to the map is the hard part. Every sun position recurs on **two dates symmetric about the solstice**, so the output must be two candidates ([Lynch](https://maths.ucd.ie/~plynch/Publications/Kingstown-Postcard.pdf)). Overcast photos, trees, scaffolding and sidewalk sheds all break the match. A semi-automatic version, where the user pins the location and marks a building corner and its shadow tip, is feasible in weeks. Full automatic registration is not.

### For NYC canopy and heat, city LiDAR beats any model you could train

Every imagery-to-shade/canopy/heat component is done. Street-view greenery and sky-view factors have Treepedia and its deep-learning successor (GVI MAE 0.047) ([GitHub](https://github.com/billcai/treepedia_dl_public)), plus Gong et al.'s sky-view factor at **R² 0.974** against manual tracing ([PDF](https://senseable.mit.edu/papers/pdf/20180425_Gong-etal_MappingSky_BuildingEnvironment.pdf)). SOLWEIG has been emulated at least three times. Shadow detection benchmarks are near-saturated, with BER under 4% on SBU/ISTD, yet methods still fail on complex real scenes ([Hu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.06998v3)). NYC also has better ground truth than any global model provides. The 2021 6-inch LiDAR land cover ships individual tree crowns over 8 ft ([Zenodo 14053441](https://zenodo.org/records/14053441)). **6,005,690 individually segmented trees** come with heights and crown areas ([Sci. Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-023-02000-w)), and the street tree census counts 666,134 trees ([NYC Open Data](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Environment/2015-Street-Tree-Census-Tree-Data/uvpi-gqnh)). Adding trees to Umbra's sun-mode shadows is data plumbing, not ML. The open questions are crown transmissivity and leaf-off months, not detection.

The omission matters. Building-only 3D sky-view factor agreed with street-view sky-view factor at only **R² 0.40**, with the largest gaps in tree-rich areas ([Gong, ICUC10](https://ams.confex.com/ams/ICUC10/mediafile/Handout/Paper343550/ICUC10_Fang-Ying%20GONG_GSV-ViewFactorMaps_Oral%20Presentation.pdf)). A Harvard field validation of a 3D sidewalk-shade model matched **91.6% on total shaded metres but only 70.4% on where along the sidewalk the shade fell** ([Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/e1f2f118-1dd5-4e39-b985-9d1c66d65f2f)). Totals-only validation flatters a model. Heat is where ambition outruns data. NYC's thermal ground truth is about 475 air-temperature sensors over two summers ([NYC Open Data](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/dataset/Hyperlocal-Temperature-Monitoring/qdq3-9eqn)) and a one-day Heat Watch traverse ([Columbia](https://news.climate.columbia.edu/2022/04/08/heat-mapping-new-york-city-environmental-justice/)). No public NYC mean-radiant-temperature or UTCI measurements were found. Air temperature responds only weakly to instantaneous shade, so correlating Umbra's heat score with those sensors would be a sanity check, not a validation. A browser Tmrt surrogate trained on SOLWEIG output can only report error against SOLWEIG. SOLWEIG's own floor against measurements is about **5.9 K RMSE**, and the U-Net emulator does slightly worse at 6.18 K ([Briegel 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212095522002772)). Replacing Umbra's WebGL building-shadow renderer with a learned surrogate would be slower and less exact than the geometry it replaces. A surrogate earns its place only for quantities that are expensive to compute: sky-view factor, longwave radiation, Tmrt, or per-edge, per-time lookups for routing.

### Remembering a hotel is table stakes in 2026; evaluated memory is not

"The assistant remembers my hotel" is a shipped consumer feature. ChatGPT added chat-history memory in April 2025 ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/May/21/chatgpt-new-memory/)). Gemini's personal context reached free users around March 2026 ([Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/03/google-gemini-past-chats-feature-free-users-rollout.html)), and Claude memory reached the free plan the same month ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/03/02/free-claude-users-can-now-use-memory-and-import-context-from-rivals/)). Google's **Ask Maps** went global in August 2026 with conversation memory and opt-in Gmail context covering "your upcoming flight or dinner reservations" ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/06/google-ask-maps-global/)). That is the hotel use case, inside the product of the employer the user is targeting. The open-source stack is equally crowded (mem0 66.5k★, Graphiti 31.4k★), and its benchmarks are compromised. Full-context GPT-4o-mini (**72.9%**) beat Mem0 (~68%) on LoCoMo ([Zep](https://www.getzep.com/blog/lies-damn-lies-statistics-is-mem0-really-sota-in-agent-memory/)). An unreviewed audit found the LoCoMo LLM judge **accepted up to 63% of deliberately wrong answers** ([Penfield](https://dev.to/penfieldlabs/we-audited-locomo-64-of-the-answer-key-is-wrong-and-the-judge-accepts-up-to-63-of-intentionally-33lg)). Plain BM25 + FAISS reached 80.1% ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46369736)).

Retrieval is the easy part. With identical retrieved evidence, knowledge-update accuracy ranged from 58% to 90% and abstention from 57% to 93% across reader models ([Memoria](https://medium.com/@matrixorigin-database/benchmarking-memoria-on-longmemeval-strong-memory-retrieval-clear-reader-separation-ee6c89c75d76), vendor post). MemoryAgentBench found no system handles conflict resolution well ([arXiv 2507.05257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05257)). Memory poisoning is a live attack class: SpAIware planted persistent memories in ChatGPT via prompt injection ([Schneier](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2024/10/hacking-chatgpt-by-planting-false-memories-into-its-data.html)). In Umbra, Foursquare and Nominatim strings are untrusted tool output that must never write memory. A credible Umbra memory stores typed slots (hotel, last location, sun tolerance, trip) in `localStorage` beside the existing `umbra:*` keys. Each slot carries Graphiti-style bi-temporal validity ([Zep paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13956)), resolved in code rather than by the LLM, and its provenance shows in the grounding receipts. A remembered sun tolerance changes the routing weight, which is the one piece Ask Maps shows no sign of doing. The whole thing is scored by a **30–50-case deterministic benchmark** covering update, contradiction, expiry, abstention, poisoning and cross-context leakage, ablated against no memory and against pasting the full history into the prompt. At a few dozen facts a vector index is unnecessary, and the benchmark should say so.

## What earns trust: baselines and error analysis, not architectures

The named practitioners agree on one point: evaluation discipline earns trust. Karpathy starts every project with "some simple model that you couldn't possibly have screwed up" and a trusted evaluation skeleton ([Karpathy](https://karpathy.github.io/2019/04/25/recipe/)). Google's Rules of ML say to design metrics first, launch without ML if needed, and keep the first model simple ([Rules of ML](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/guides/rules-of-ml)). Husain and Shankar call **error analysis "the most important activity in evals"** ([hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/)). Eugene Yan's rubric probes data literacy and evaluation, and treats "lack of curiosity about unexpected results" as a red flag ([eugeneyan.com](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/how-to-interview/)).

On tabular data, tree ensembles remain state of the art at medium scale ([Grinsztajn et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08815)), so picking XGBoost or a Bayesian logit where it fits reads as judgment, provided the write-up says why. Two methodology points follow. Any ML component should be small enough that every number, split and failure can be defended. And random train/test splits leak through spatial autocorrelation, so any per-segment or per-tile model needs **borough- or neighbourhood-blocked splits**. That generalizes Kapoor and Narayanan's leakage taxonomy ([Patterns](https://www.cell.com/patterns/fulltext/S2666-3899(23)00159-9)).

## Ranking the additions: three foundations, then one small model of Umbra's own

The ranking scores novelty, feasibility for one person in weeks, and signal (how much a result demonstrates), each from 1 to 5, multiplies the three, and then applies judgment. The non-ML foundations come first because every ML addition depends on them for credibility. Pricing exposure at traversal time without measuring the shadow field's error, or learning a preference over an unvalidated exposure number, compounds unknown error.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Assistant reliability report**: τ-bench-style scenarios graded on final app state (pins, route, time), pass^k over k live Gemini runs, failure taxonomy | Eval (LLM) | 2×5×5 | pass^1 vs pass^k per scenario; tool-argument correctness; run-to-run variance; categorized failures | Its own pass^1. Report the drop honestly, as τ-bench did for GPT-4o (~60%) |
| F2 | **Shadow-reality audit + LiDAR trees**: direct-beam shade from the NYC LiDAR DSM vs Umbra's rendered mask; add TNC 2021 crowns / Ma et al. trees | Eval + data | 4×5×4 | Mask IoU and per-sidewalk-segment shade-fraction error, reported as a **distribution, not totals**; split by building-only vs with-trees, hour, borough | Umbra building-only. Sets the error bar no competitor publishes |
| F3 | **Traversal-time pricing ablation + exact bi-objective oracle** | Algorithm | 4×4×4 | Over N random OD pairs × departure times: % of routes that change, exposure mis-estimate of departure-time pricing, growth with trip length and low sun; Pareto recall vs exact oracle | Departure-time pricing (shadewalker's method); Li et al.'s 2019 single static number |
| 1 | **Hierarchical Bayesian shade-preference model**: refit Melnikov's OSF logit, use it as the population prior, update per user from Pareto picks and the stated sun tolerance | Classical/Bayesian ML | 4×4×4 = 64 | Reproduce β̄ = 1.16; held-out choice log-likelihood and accuracy with **participant-blocked** splits; calibration; simulated-user α recovery vs number of picks | Population-mean α; fixed ladders (shadewalker 0/5/15/40); a non-hierarchical logit |

| 3 | **Semi-automatic shadow chronolocation**: user pins place and marks corner + shadow tip; sweep time with suncalc + renderer; return two date candidates or abstain | Geometric CV (+ optional VLM tie-break) | 3×3×4 = 36 | Self-collected NYC photo set with trusted EXIF time, stratified by sun elevation and season, including overcast (must abstain): median minutes error, % within 15/30 min, date-candidate hit rate, abstention accuracy | A Gemini vision time guess on the same photos (cheap, same key); GT-Loc's published 2.72 h; a human doing the ShadeMap workflow |

| 5 | **NYC Tmrt/UTCI browser surrogate** trained on SOLWEIG over NYC LiDAR, exported to ONNX Runtime Web | Deep learning | 3×2×3 = 18 | Error vs SOLWEIG on held-out tiles and days; latency on named hardware; UI must say it emulates a model | Briegel U-Net (2.4 K MAE) and GSM-UTCI (0.41 °C) on their own terms. **No NYC measured truth exists** |

Two items lead the ML ranking, and they compose into one story. The preference model (rank 1) is the only addition that is simultaneously novel, small, trainable on public data, and wired into a decision the product makes. No shade product learns preference. The prior comes from a published, reproducible study. Google's own world-scale IRL work ([RHIP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11290)) is the industrial analogue. Its honest weakness is data. With no accounts and few picks per user, the online part can be validated only in simulation until real usage exists, and 46 Singaporeans in a courtyard are a domain shift from Manhattan in July. The write-up should say both. Memory (rank 2) scores low on novelty because the feature is commodity, but it is cheap, it covers the GenAI keyword, and it becomes distinctive when a stated preference ("I hate long sunny stretches") enters the Bayesian model as a prior that behaviour then updates. Chronolocation (rank 3) is the demo, the PIGEON-style "try it in a minute" artifact. It is legitimately differentiated in execution. It should process photos locally and never store them, because photo geolocation carries a doxxing risk ([arXiv 2502.11163](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11163v2)).

The lower ranks deserve a blunt reading. The per-edge surrogate and the Tmrt surrogate look the most like "real ML", meaning a neural net or GBDT trained on hundreds of thousands of examples. That is their pull for someone who wants an obviously-ML artifact. But each has a strong non-ML competitor, a lookup table or SOLWEIG itself, and a validation ceiling set by the label. Built without beating that competitor, either one reproduces the "model without a baseline" red flag it was meant to avoid. The do-not-build list is long and specific: a learned replacement for the WebGL shadow renderer, a shadow detector trained on SBU/ISTD, a canopy-height, GVI or building-height model for NYC, a planet-scale geolocation model, learned walking ETA, world-scale IRL without traces, grid Neural A*, and a vector-database memory layer. Each is solved, mismatched to Umbra's data, or beaten by a baseline Umbra already has.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which model should Umbra add?" to "what number can Umbra stand behind that no competitor publishes?" Shadewalker, ShadeMap, openrouteservice and Google have between them covered the shade-routing feature surface. None of them has published a measured shade error, a time-dependence ablation, or a learned preference. Those three are cheap and honest, and they are what ML practitioners say they evaluate. The ML that follows is then small by design. A Bayesian preference model fed by the assistant's memory is a few hundred lines of code, and it can be defended number by number.

One second-order implication is worth acting on early. Umbra's real moat against shadewalker.nyc is the error bar, not the feature list. Shadewalker is AGPL, covers more of NYC at finer temporal resolution, and validates only against its own goldens. The first NYC shade router to publish "our shade is right X% of the time, here is where it is wrong, and here is what trees add" redefines the comparison on its own terms, and makes every later ML claim credible by inheritance. A full-feature transit-plus-rain-plus-assistant app without that number reads as one more entry in a crowded hobby category.
