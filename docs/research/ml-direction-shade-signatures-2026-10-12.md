# Evaluate the shade table, not the embedding

PCA + k-means is already the right model for Umbra's shade signatures, and no learned embedding is likely to beat it. The ML work worth doing sits on either side of the signature. On one side is a task-shaped assistant tool that answers time-window shade questions straight from the exact table, with a measured evaluation behind it. On the other is a study that uses the exact table as ground truth for things that are genuinely uncertain, starting with whether street imagery can see shade at all. The candid ranking has three options. **First, a `find_shade_spots` / shade-calendar tool** with the full agent-evaluation battery (pass^k, should-not-call cases, a tool-removal ablation, exact claim receipts). It answers questions the live canvas cannot, such as "where can I wait in shade from 2 to 4 pm on a July Tuesday?" **Second, an imagery-versus-exact-shade validation study.** This replaces the originally planned "SigLIP judges vibe" step with "SigLIP is measured against physics". **Third, a residual surrogate** that asks how much of exact shade an idealised street-canyon formula explains and what a learned model adds. Yes, the interview story should be about evaluation. The most interview-ready fact Umbra already owns is that **exact recall@10 of 0.41 looks like failure but hides a neighbour-distance ratio of 1.07**. That is a textbook "misleading summary statistic", and explaining why is worth more than any autoencoder. Of the planned phases, route explanations survive but should quote table hours rather than types. "Streets like this one" shrinks to a secondary mode of the shade tool. City-scale Find the Light keeps its candidate search but loses SigLIP as a judge until the validation study earns it a place. The shipped street-type layer should come out of the user-facing UI.

## The data rules out every fashionable embedding

The table is about 3.2M sidewalk sides × 768 deterministic physics values: 12 representative days × 64 fifteen-minute slots, about 581 of them in daylight. The values carry no noise, no labels and no natural augmentation. Every learned representation method that looks impressive on paper wins under the opposite conditions. The canonical comparison of dimensionality-reduction methods found that nonlinear techniques "do not outperform the traditional PCA on real-world tasks" ([van der Maaten et al., via OpenAlex](https://openalex.org/W2137570937)). For an L2 notion of similarity, truncated PCA is the optimal rank-16 linear projection, so an autoencoder can only "win" by bending the metric, which changes what "similar" means. TS2Vec's gains are on UCR/UEA *classification*, where invariance to shifts and noise pays ([TS2Vec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10466)). Contrastive methods need augmentations to define those invariances, and jittering an exact table teaches invariance to noise that does not exist.

Time-series foundation models fare worse. MOMENT's zero-shot embeddings scored a mean **0.794** on 91 UCR datasets, below TS2Vec's **0.851**. MOMENT also normalises each input, so it "cannot differentiate between vertically shifted time series" ([MOMENT](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03885v3)). For shade fractions, the absolute level is the main signal. In StarEmbed's favourable setting of noisy real light curves, 138 hand-crafted features still beat Chronos on k-means NMI (**0.270 vs 0.237**) and kNN macro-F1 (**0.712 vs 0.672**) ([StarEmbed](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06200)). On a clean physics table, PCA plays the hand-crafted role. Metric learning is a weak bet even with labels: once methods were compared fairly, its gains were "marginal at best" ([Musgrave et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08505)). Umbra has no labels for it anyway.

Umbra's own phase-0 numbers (`docs/handoffs/SHADE_SIGNATURES.md`) point to two fixes that need no new model. **Recall@10 = 0.41 with a 1.07 distance ratio** is the signature of a dense continuum with many near-ties. The cheap fix is to shortlist the top ~100 in PCA space and re-rank by exact distance on the 581 daylight values, the refine stage FAISS formalises as `RFlat` ([FAISS guidelines](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss/wiki/Guidelines-to-choose-an-index)). Exact recall should then approach 1 at trivial cost, though that has not been measured yet. **Silhouette 0.21, falling as K rises**, says the clusters are bins, not discovered types. They are still real bins: every cluster is far tighter than size-matched random groups (z 17–114). So k-means is right as *vector quantisation into archetypes* and wrong as a claim about natural street kinds. HDBSCAN would mostly label a continuum as noise ([HDBSCAN docs](https://hdbscan.readthedocs.io/en/latest/comparing_clustering_algorithms.html)).

If interpretability matters, there are two cheaper upgrades: NMF, whose non-negative parts give soft memberships directly, and varimax-rotated PCA axes ("overall shadiness", "AM vs PM", "seasonal"). Brute force is also the right search. Single-query exact search over 3.2M × 16 floats took about **113 ms** in plain numpy on the research laptop. FAISS's HNSW memory formula puts a d=16, M=32 index at about 1 GB, against 205 MB flat ([FAISS](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss/wiki/Guidelines-to-choose-an-index)). In-browser HNSW or WebGPU search at this size is padding.

What makes the existing model defensible is a few days of evaluation, not a new architecture. Before any option below, Umbra should run a battery on spatially blocked samples, because adjacent sidewalks are near-duplicates and inflate every score. The battery has six parts:

1. Recall@10 and distance ratio, before and after exact re-rank.
2. Trustworthiness at k=10, which penalises false neighbours by rank ([scikit-learn](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.manifold.trustworthiness.html)).
3. Bootstrap and seed ARI of the K=8 partition.
4. Probes from bearing, canyon height-to-width ratio and borough to each component.
5. A **route-substitution test**: swap each segment's profile for its 1-NN's and measure the change in route shade score and in the Pareto route chosen.
6. Equal-dimension ablations of NMF, walker-weighted PCA (columns scaled by when people walk, then PCA) and a small autoencoder.

The route-substitution metric is Umbra's own proposal; no published version was found. It is the only one of the six that measures whether the signature preserves what the product cares about. The most likely outcome is "PCA held; the AE moved nothing that matters". Reported with numbers, that is a stronger result than shipping the AE.

## Three ranked options, scored against interviews and the assistant

The options differ in where the "ML" lives. In Option 1 it is a retrieval layer and an agent-evaluation program. In Option 2 it is a supervised probe evaluated against exact ground truth. In Option 3 it is a tabular model evaluated against physics. Effort figures are this report's estimates for one person working with an agent, not measurements.

| Rank | Option | What users get | Assistant tool | Interview fit | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shade-window tool + agent eval | "Shadiest (or sunniest) spot to wait near X, 2–4 pm on date D"; "when is this block shaded" | `find_shade_spots` (modes: area, street side, similar streets) | AI/agent engineering: strong. Classic ML: moderate | 2–3 weeks |
| 2 | Imagery vs exact-shade validation | Indirectly: a published error bar, canopy gaps found, a Find the Light reranker only if it earns one | Optional rerank inside tool 1; no new tool | Applied ML / Geo ML: strongest | 3–5 weeks |
| 3 | Residual shade surrogate | Nothing in NYC; an attribution phrase at most | None credible | Tabular ML with spatial CV: solid but generic | 2–3 weeks |

### Option 1 answers questions five competitors cannot

Users can already get "which side is shaded now" and "a shady route" from Stay Cool ("live in 39 cities", including NYC) ([Stay Cool](https://www.walkintheshade.com/about)), inShade ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/inshade-shady-walk-routes/id6779049186)) and shadewalker.nyc. Shadewalker computes a 12 × 24 month-by-hour table internally but never shows it ([shadewalker](https://github.com/camrynobscura/shadewalker)). The unserved shapes are the per-place sun timeline that terrace apps such as SunSeekr proved for venues ([SunSeekr](https://www.sunseekr.com/)), applied to sidewalks, and "where can I wait in shade at time T", which is shipped only for stadium seats ([isthereshade.com](https://www.isthereshade.com/)). The behavioural evidence says the value is in *revealing where shade exists*. In Tel Aviv, the share of pedestrians in shade rose from **44% to 80%** as shade availability rose ([Buildings & Cities](https://journal-buildingscities.org/articles/10.5334/bc.574)). In Hiroshima, the busier side of wide streets flipped between summer and fall ([J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/aijt/30/76/30_1524/_article/-char/en)). The tool therefore needs a sun-seeking mode, not just "shadiest".

The tool should be one task-shaped call, not raw vector search. It would look like `find_shade_spots({lat, lng, from, to, date?, radiusM: 200|400|800, mode: "shade"|"sun", street?, likeCandidateId?})`. It returns at most four results, each labelled "W 4th St, north side", with `meanShade`, an hour-by-hour list, a `candidateId` for `plot_points` and `plan_shadowed_route`, and a `basis` string such as "typical day, July 15 (nearest representative day)". The core ranking is filter-and-sort over the raw table, with no embedding. The signature appears only in the `likeCandidateId` mode, as a PCA shortlist followed by exact re-rank, with the reference street's hours returned beside each match so similarity is shown rather than asserted.

Every vendor's guidance points this way. Anthropic says tools that "merely wrap existing software functionality or API endpoints" are a common mistake ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/writing-tools-for-agents)). OpenAI says "Don't make the model fill arguments you already know" ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/function-calling)). Google recommends strong typing and a 10–20 tool ceiling ([Gemini](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/function-calling)). The Umbra-specific reason is the step budget. A raw-kNN chain (geocode → get_signature → knn → check each → plot) would blow through `MAX_STEPS` 8, and success compounds downward with steps: at 95% per step, about 60% survive ten steps ([Huyen](https://huyenchip.com/2025/01/07/agents.html)). The description must separate the tool from `check_shadow`: "use `check_shadow` for right now on the visible map; use `find_shade_spots` for a time window or another date". Overlap, not tool count, is the real selection risk ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)).

Grounding slots into what exists. `verifyAnswer()` in `app/lib/agent/receipts.ts` already rejects a shadow claim unless the fraction equals the observation. A new `shade_window` claim kind gets the same exact check. A regex sweep over `text` blocks catches stray numbers and clock times, which the schema already forbids there. Numeric graders need stated tolerance rules: Anthropic reports a CORE-Bench score rising from 42% to 95% once grader bugs were fixed, including one that rejected "96.12" against "96.124991…" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)).

The evaluation is where this option becomes interview material. It has six parts:

1. **Hermetic tests of the tool alone.** Hour values equal the table, results are sorted, and windows clip at sunset. Recall@k of shortlist + re-rank is measured against brute force.
2. **New `eval:agent` scenarios.** Ten to fifteen of them, including 3–4 should-not-call cases ("is it shaded *right now*" belongs to `check_shadow`; an out-of-NYC query must degrade to an `unknown` block). This mirrors BFCL's irrelevance category ([BFCL](https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html)).
3. **pass^k at k=3.** τ-bench showed pass^1 overstates reliability: GPT-4o fell from 61% at pass^1 to about 25% at pass^8 on retail ([τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)).
4. **A tool-selection confusion matrix**, plus invalid-parameter and incorrect-value rates.
5. **An ablation with the tool removed.** The agent falls back to `search_places` + `check_shadow`; report the change in success, steps and latency. This is Huyen's "if a tool can be removed without a performance drop, remove it" ([Huyen](https://huyenchip.com/2025/01/07/agents.html)).
6. **A product baseline.** Compare "nearest sidewalk" with "shadiest within radius" as shade gained per extra metre walked.

To avoid tuning the tool description to Manhattan, split the scenario set by borough and hold one borough out. The same harness can settle an open tension cheaply. Google now "strongly recommend[s]" temperature 1.0 for Gemini 3 and warns that lower values can cause looping ([Gemini 3 guide](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/gemini-3)), while Umbra runs at 0. A pass^k A/B on the same scenarios turns that into a measured decision. The honest weakness is that the ML content is thin: a filter, a sort and a re-ranked kNN. It sells to AI-engineering roles, where evals are the job; Husain and Shankar report spending "60–80% of our development time on error analysis and evaluation" ([AI Evals FAQ](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/)). It sells less well to an ML-modelling panel.

### Option 2 turns the exact table into ground truth nobody else has

Measuring sky-view factor and green-view index from street imagery is mature. CLIP/VLM scoring of street qualities exists in UrbanCLIP, WalkCLIP and SAGAI ([UrbanCLIP](https://doi.org/10.1080/13658816.2024.2347322); [WalkCLIP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21947); [SAGAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20026)). Street-view sun-duration work has been validated only at point stations or field sites ([Hong Kong, B&E 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.buildenv.2018.10.025)). A 2024 Paris shaded-routing demo extracts shade with segmentation foundation models and reports no accuracy against geometric shadow at all ([arXiv 2407.13689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13689)). An EGU 2026 abstract on deep-learning shade detection from street imagery was withdrawn ([EGU26-8982](https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu26-8982)). No study was found that validates image-derived shade against a dense, exact, time-resolved ground truth. Umbra has one.

The experiment needs no scraping. Global Streetscapes ships **18,420 New York images** under CC BY-SA 4.0, with per-image local datetime and lighting and glare labels ([HF dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NUS-UAL/global-streetscapes)).

The labels, in order of value:

1. **Instantaneous shade at capture time.** The table, the `captured_at` timestamp and the matched sidewalk side together say whether that side was in shade when the photo was taken. Cast shadows can be checked against the pixels, so the label runs both ways. Disagreements expose errors in the *table*, such as trees missing from the canopy raster, as well as errors in the model.
2. Summer-midday shade fraction.
3. Annual shade fraction.
4. Signature archetype.

The model ladder, cheapest first:

1. An analytic infinite-canyon baseline from height-to-width ratio and bearing.
2. A one-feature regression on segmentation-derived SVF/GVI, the literature standard.
3. A zero-shot SigLIP/CLIP prompt score.
4. A linear probe on frozen SigLIP or DINOv2 embeddings.
5. GBT on geometry plus image features.

The question that matters is whether the image adds anything beyond cheap geometry. Metrics are Spearman ρ for the uncalibrated zero-shot scores, R² for the probes, AUROC for the instantaneous label and for top-versus-bottom shade quartile, and nDCG for within-neighbourhood ranking.

The split protocol decides whether the result is believed. Ploton et al. showed a random forest that appeared to explain over half of biomass variance under random CV had "quasi-null predictive power" under spatial CV ([Nat Commun 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-18321-y)). Folds should be spatial blocks of at least 500 m, which is this report's inference from winter tower shadows. Both sides of a segment, and every image from one capture sequence, go in the same fold. Leave-one-borough-out is a second, harder test. Report random and blocked CV side by side, because the gap is itself a finding. kNNDM is the principled middle ground for fold design ([GMD 2024](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-17-5897-2024)). Wadoux et al.'s counterpoint is that design-based validation beats spatial CV when a probability sample exists ([Ecol Model 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2021.109692)). It deserves one sentence in the write-up, since Global Streetscapes is not a probability sample.

The risks are real. Volunteer imagery clusters on local roads, among a few prolific contributors, and at certain times of day ([IJGI 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijgi9060341)). Leaf-off winter images understate canopy. Mapillary's terms forbid unapproved "data mining" and reportedly restrict "real-time navigation" use ([Mapillary terms](https://www.mapillary.com/terms)); the second clause's wording was not confirmed. The study should therefore stay offline, and any image-derived score should not drive live routing until the terms are read in full.

The assistant tie-in is honest but modest. If, and only if, the probe shows image scores track the table, a SigLIP score becomes an optional rerank inside `find_shade_spots` ("leafy", "quiet"). It is then evaluated as "did reranking cost any shade?". This option produces the strongest interview artifact for applied-ML and Geo roles: a novel ground truth, a baseline ladder, leakage-safe splits, and error analysis that finds bugs in the author's own data.

### Option 3 is good tabular ML with no product attached

Emulators of SOLWEIG-class physics work: a U-Net reproduced pedestrian-level mean radiant temperature at **2.4 K MAE, 22× faster** than SOLWEIG ([Urban Climate 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.uclim.2022.101359)). But Umbra already has the exact answer for NYC, and GPU SOLWEIG now runs over "tens to hundreds of square kilometers" ([GLIDE-SOL](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2026-776/)), so speed is no motive. The defensible framing is a *residual* model. Start with the analytic canyon shade fraction from height-to-width ratio, bearing, side and latitude. Fit a GBT on cheap features (OSM heights, width, canopy %), then a GBT on the residual. The finding is how much of exact shade the idealised canyon explains, and how much of the remainder (irregular skylines, gaps, intersections, canopy) a model recovers. Physics alone is strongly structured: in Ghent, daily mean radiant temperature fell exponentially with height-to-width ratio and about 2.5× faster in north–south streets ([ICUC12](https://doi.org/10.5194/icuc12-645)).

Leakage is the trap. Any ray-cast feature computed from the same building model (sky-view factor, horizon angles, sun-hours) puts the target into the inputs. Evaluation is leave-one-borough-out, residuals by bearing, height-to-width ratio, canopy and season, and an area-of-applicability flag for predictions outside the training feature space ([Meyer & Pebesma](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210x.13650)). A GNN is the wrong inductive bias here: shade depends on buildings within a few hundred metres, not on graph topology, and trees still beat deep models on typical tabular data ([Grinsztajn et al.](https://doi.org/10.52202/068431-0037)). This option ranks third because Umbra's data is NYC-only, so the model has no user anywhere. Training on NYC and testing on another city would require running the exact pipeline in that city, which makes the model redundant exactly where it is tested. Its best use is as a fallback if Option 2 stalls on imagery coverage or licensing. Learning-to-rank does not appear among the options: every ranking Umbra performs is exact arithmetic over the table, and learning a human shade preference needs choice data Umbra does not have.

## Phase-by-phase verdicts on the original signature plan

The original handoff planned four product phases on top of the signatures. The evidence keeps two in altered form, demotes one and rewrites the last.

| Phase | Verdict | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| 3. Street-type map layer (shipped) | **Remove from user UI** | Keep the projection code only if it costs nothing. At most, expose the layer behind a dev flag as a QA view for Option 2 disagreements |
| 4. Route explanations | **Survives, changed** | Generate phrases from raw table hours ("shaded until 9:45 am, sunny at midday"), each one a receipt. Never put type names in user copy |
| 5. Assistant "streets like this one" | **Demoted** | Becomes the `likeCandidateId` mode of `find_shade_spots`, with exact re-rank and side-by-side hours. Lowest priority: no shipped product or paper offering it was found, so demand is unproven |
| 6. City-scale Find the Light with SigLIP | **Survives, rewired** | Candidates come from the raw table (window filter plus sun-seeking mode), not from signatures. SigLIP stops being a judge and becomes Option 2's subject. It returns as a reranker only if the probe beats geometry under blocked CV |

The layer's case is weak on three counts. First, it barely discriminates where users are: **70% of Midtown street sides and 60% of avenue sides are one type** (handoff, phase 0). Second, it is static under a time slider, which is Umbra's central interaction. Third, it invites the "discovered types" overclaim that silhouette 0.21 cannot support. Opposite sidewalks also land in different types 36–53% of the time, so any segment-level reading is wrong half the time. A tap-to-see shade calendar on any sidewalk side gives the same information in a form users can act on. Route explanations need no signature at all. The archetype label can appear in an explanation only as a compact tag *beside* the hours that justify it.

## What the interview story should actually be

The research converges on one claim: the credible story is about judgment and measurement, not the embedding. Eugene Yan's interview guide probes "alternative designs or techniques considered", how the eval harness was built, and "What was a misleading summary statistic?" ([Yan](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/how-to-interview/)). Umbra can answer all three from work largely done. The signature itself is the "alternatives considered" answer: autoencoders, TS2Vec, Chronos and MOMENT were rejected for stated reasons, and the cheap ablation can prove the call. Recall 0.41 versus ratio 1.07 is the misleading statistic. Option 1's harness is the eval answer. On novelty, the honest claim is the *artifact*, not the method. Searches found no published per-sidewalk-side, time × season shade typology, and mainstream street embeddings such as Street2Vec are built to be "invariant to seasonal and daily changes" ([Street2Vec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11354)). That absence rests on degraded search (OpenAlex and arXiv probes, no Google Scholar), so phrase it as "no equivalent found".

Two phrasings to avoid, because a probing interviewer will break them: "AI-powered shade intelligence", and k-means archetypes presented as discovered street types. A natural question is whether this displaces a trained model of Umbra's own. It does not. If a panel wants "trained a model, beat a baseline on held-out data", Option 2's probe is that model, and its baseline ladder is the evidence.

## Conclusion

The shade-signature project has already produced its most valuable ML result, and it is a negative one: on an exact physics table, the classical compressor is optimal and the fashionable alternatives have nothing to learn. What remains is to stop asking the embedding to carry product value it cannot carry. The value lives in the raw table, served through one well-evaluated tool, and in the table's unusual status as dense ground truth for methods that are otherwise validated only at a handful of points. Build Option 1 first, because it changes what the assistant can do this month and its evaluation reuses infrastructure that already exists. Run the representation battery alongside it, because it takes days. Start Option 2 once Option 1 ships, because it is the piece no competitor or paper has, and it can find errors in Umbra's own canopy data.

The uncertainties are worth stating plainly. No user research shows that NYC walkers want shade calendars or similarity search; demand is inferred from terrace and stadium apps. The re-rank's recall gain and the route-substitution metric are proposals, not measurements. Mapillary's navigation clause is unverified. The novelty claims rest on degraded search tools. Each of these can be checked cheaply, and the write-up should name each one that remains unchecked.
