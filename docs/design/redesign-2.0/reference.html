Umbra Redesign 2.0

The visual language Umbra is moving to: occult civic ephemera printed on warm black. This page is the shared reference for every session that works on the redesign. It records the language (principles, tokens, type, composition, texture, motion, voice), how it maps onto Umbra's data, and the order of work.

In one sentence

A city told through its printed paper

Umbra redesign 2.0 presents the city the way the city documents itself: postcards, transit maps, timetables, guidebooks, stamped tickets and shop catalogues. Everything sits on a warm near-black (or its paper inverse), is inked in cream, and is sorted by color the way a transit system sorts lines.

1

Every surface borrows a printed format

A route is a transit strip, a trip summary is a postcard, a place is a guidebook entry, the hourly forecast is a timetable. The format tells people how to read the surface before they read it.

2

Never pure black, never pure white

Ground #070707 or green-tinted #10130D; ink cream #F7E9D5 / #FFEFD7. In daylight the roles invert onto paper. Pure #000 and #FFF never appear.

3

Color is a taxonomy

Every meaning owns a hue, and every hue ships as a five-step ramp (light, bright, base, dark, darker). Chrome borrows no hue it does not mean.

4

Hand-placed, slightly wrong

Plates are irregular quadrilaterals. Labels sit 0.2° to 7° off square. Shadows are hard offsets with zero blur, like a pasted cutout or a misregistered print.

5

Two voices of type, plus lettering

A chiseled display face for names and titles, a wide stamped grotesque for labels, a clean geometric body. Special moments get bespoke lettering.

6

Texture is print damage

Film grain, halftone dots, speckle inside type, torn and scratched edges, paper and chalk. Applied to UI surfaces, never over data.

7

One recurring mark

A single motif repeats across logo, markers and states. For Umbra it is the umbra disc: a sun partly covered by its own shadow.

8

Readability beats spectacle

A stated read hierarchy, cool shadows instead of black ones, rim separation, limited emissive colors, customizable danger colors. Ornament yields whenever it competes with a number.

9

A deadpan tour guide

Headings pair a brochure kicker with a name. Warmth and wit live in kickers and empty states; numbers stay plain and sourced.

Composition

Layout, plates and the pasted look

Poster canvas (marketing pages only) Measured

  • The root is a container defining --s: calc(min(100cqw, 1500px) / 1876). Elements take inline --x --y --w --h --r in design units, turned into left/top/width/height × --s and rotate.
  • Bands are bounded by y0/y1. Full-bleed art escapes with left: calc(50% - 50cqw); width: 100cqw.
  • This scales as one image and does not reflow: at 400px, body copy falls near 6px. It suits a showcase page. Umbra's app must not use it; the About page may, with a reflowing phone layout.

Rotation Measured

  • Web: 44 rotated placements, 0.2° to 6.9°, most often 1.2–1.6°. Highlight swatches at −1.8° under text at −0.38°.
  • UI: −3° and −1° most common, then 2°, −5°, −10°; skew(-10deg) for highlight bars; stacked bars at −4°.

Shadows Measured

  • Objects: 16 16 0 rgba(0,0,0,.25); also 12, 10 and 8. Zero blur everywhere. Display type takes the same offset as a text-shadow.
  • Cutouts use drop-shadow so the shadow follows the silhouette.

Plates and badges Measured

  • Every plate is a quadrilateral whose corners wander. On a 442×111 plate the corners sit at (12.8, 110.9), (0, 27.4), (434.6, 0), (442, 76.4).
  • Dark plates: fill in the darkened line hue, 8px inner stroke of the accent at 10%, a drop plus inner shadow.
  • Number badges are circles masked by a rough stamped texture so the rim chips. Line badges are pills with a letter in a circle and an ink-rough rim.
  • Routes on maps: dashed lines in the accent at 10–30%, stops as ringed dots (signal orange rim, dark fill, hard shadow).

Component vocabulary Measured

Kicker, Highlight, Blurb (kicker → title → body), Badge, Callout, Plate, TornPanel, PaintBg, SmallCard, Tile (looping muted clip with a poster frame), Lightbox, Panorama.

Materials and texture

Web

  • A grain photo over headers; a per-section grain with mix-blend-mode: exclusion at 50%
  • A gray newspaper and poster collage behind feature sections, lit with color-dodge and screen glows
  • A 2000×80 torn edge repeated between bands, flipped for the opposite edge
  • City skyline silhouettes per category
  • Kraft-paper notes, torn card panels, a folded paper map
  • Photo toning: sepia(0.8) brightness(1.33) at 50%

UI

  • Paper tiles, worn paper overlays per card
  • Film grain, card grain, a print-screen overlay, a halftone dot backer
  • Speckle masks inside type
  • Rough edges and ink-threshold masks for reveals
  • Chalk strokes and a blackboard mask; a chalk variant of each badge
  • Card backers per category and tier
Timing

Motion

Web: a still poster

  • No scroll animation or parallax.
  • Motion lives in looping, muted clips with poster frames.
  • Hover brightens an 8-unit frame from cream 50% to 100% over 0.15s.
  • Lightbox on 85% black with the same hard shadow.

UI: short, stamped, masked

  • Durations: 0.2s most, then 0.1s and 0.3s; ease-in-out.
  • Named moves: stamp, mask wipe, logo reveal, pulse and glow for alerts.
  • Reveals use ink-threshold masks, so things appear printed or burned in, not faded.
  • Emissive colors are limited to a handful of meanings.
Recurring marks

Motifs and iconography

  • One motif, everywhere. The reference system repeats a single watching mark across logo, status bars, creatures and signage. Umbra's equivalent is the umbra disc (sun with its shadow bite), used in the logo, the now-marker, the user dot and the arrival stamp.
  • Transit bullets. A letter or number in a circle inside a pill, in the line color, for lines and categories.
  • Category sigils. Each category owns one simple geometric glyph (crosshair, plus-cross, pentagram in the reference; sun, tree, umbrella, train for Umbra).
  • Roman numerals for tiers and difficulty; starbursts for featured items.
  • Screenprint icons: black silhouette, one accent color, paper grain; two to three colors.
  • Diagrams as ornament: radial circles and clock faces behind portraits. For Umbra, the sun path diagram.
Illustration and map

Rendering rules

Rules for any illustrated asset and for the basemap style. They come from the lineage of the reference system's shading and suit a product about shade.

  1. Shading moves from warm to cool. Shadows go cool, never black.
  2. Saturation rises where light turns to shadow; that edge is often warmed.
  3. Drop high-frequency detail. Suggest repetition instead of drawing every window.
  4. Interior details echo the outer silhouette.
  5. Separate figure from ground with rim light first; outlines only where rim light fails.
  6. Muted fields, small saturated accents. Full saturation is reserved for things that matter to the user.
  7. State a read hierarchy and map one channel to each level: meaning by hue, kind by silhouette, urgency by local contrast.
  8. Cut effects that compete with data; keep only the ones that inform.
Copy

Voice

The reference writes as a visitor's guide. Headings are a brochure kicker plus a name ("See the sights on / Main Street", "Mind the / Loose Cobbles", "Wait, what happened? / Trip Log"). Bodies set up civic normality and end on a sting.

For Umbra the kicker carries the personality and the rest stays plain. Examples:

  • "Keep cool on / Broadway" over a route card whose verdict still reads "18 min, 86% shaded".
  • "Greetings from / the shady side" on the arrival postcard, above "2 of 6 min in sun at a fixed 5.0 km/h pace".
  • "Nothing saved yet / Your walks will live here" for the saved-routes empty state.

Umbra's copy rules still govern: verdict first, every number traceable with its uncertainty, no marketing adjectives, an 11px floor for captions.

Theme follows the sun UmbraDecide

Day theme (paper ground, warm ink) while the sun is up; night theme (warm black, cream) after sunset, driven by the app's own solar model at the selected time, with a manual override. Night shows only when the sun is down, and with the sun down there are no shadows, so the dark basemap never has to carry shadow pixels. Outdoor daylight use keeps the high-glare-friendly light ground.

What stays fixed

  • Shadow detection couples to shadow color: the painted shadow must stay blue-dominant after compositing over whichever basemap ships. Re-verify after every basemap or palette change.
  • 44px touch targets, 11px caption floor, phone-first at 390×844, sunlight legibility.
  • Tilt, texture and plates are decoration. Numbers, inputs, map controls and anything aligned in columns stay square, untextured and unrotated.
  • Texture never sits over the map canvas.
  • Focus is always visible, including on clipped plates (use an inner stroke; outlines get clipped).
  • Reduced motion removes stamps and wipes; reduced transparency removes grain.

For every session

Rules for sessions

  • Call this work "Umbra redesign 2.0" in code, commits, PRs, issues and docs. Do not name any outside product, studio or platform as its inspiration.
  • Use no third-party brand assets, fonts or artwork. Only openly licensed fonts and original drawings.
  • Until R0 merges, Canopy (docs/design/language.md) is the binding spec.
  • Every PR in the wave carries day and night phone shots, runs the interface reviewer, and runs the grounding audit whenever copy or numbers change.
